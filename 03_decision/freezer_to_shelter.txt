Freezer-to-shelter analogy (qualitative explanation; no project calculation)

A freezer provides useful service by maintaining stored contents within a desired
temperature range while heat enters through its envelope, openings, and contents.
Its electrical input drives a refrigeration process. A shelter's useful service
is occupied thermal comfort under outdoor weather, radiant exposure, air exchange,
and internal heat and moisture sources. Open boundaries and movement matter.

The analogy organizes service, environmental load, and energy input; it does not
make the two services thermodynamically identical. Fan A changes bodily heat transfer
through local airspeed; it does not refrigerate the shelter air by 3 degrees.
Direct evaporative B/C lower supply dry-bulb temperature while increasing humidity.
Their useful effect depends on inlet conditions, ventilation, exposure, and people.

Occupied person-degree-hours of avoided SET hot discomfort per electrical kWh is
a service-efficiency metric, not thermodynamic COP. A mixing or coverage factor
does not directly measure wasted electrical energy. Coverage represented through
occupied zones must not be multiplied into benefit a second time.

No numerical comparison, adequacy finding, safety classification, or recommendation
is possible until the readiness report is resolved.
